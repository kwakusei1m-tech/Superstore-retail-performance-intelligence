# AI evaluation interview drills

## 60-second positioning
“I design evaluation tasks around real business outcomes. My EVAL-OPS process establishes the capability and risk, versions the environment, authors self-contained tasks, locks observable rubrics, runs repeatable trials, records evidence-linked errors and standardizes reviewers through calibration and regression testing. I demonstrate it with a governed Superstore Excel model and its linked Word report and executive PowerPoint.”

## Timed drills

1. **15 minutes - Ambiguity audit:** rewrite a vague task so two reviewers would interpret it consistently.
2. **20 minutes - Rubric design:** create five weighted, non-overlapping criteria with pass/partial/fail anchors.
3. **20 minutes - Pairwise grading:** score two answers independently, choose a winner and cite decisive evidence.
4. **15 minutes - Observation writing:** use Observation -> Evidence -> Criterion -> Severity -> Impact -> Correction.
5. **15 minutes - Grader choice:** explain which checks should be deterministic, expert-human or model-judged.
6. **20 minutes - Error clustering:** group ten failures, identify root cause and propose dataset/prompt/rubric changes.
7. **20 minutes - Calibration:** explain a reviewer disagreement and update one boundary example.

## Likely interview questions

- How do you know the task measures the intended capability rather than prompt ambiguity?
- How do you prevent rubric overlap and hidden criteria?
- When do you grade the final state versus the agent trajectory?
- What would make you reject or revise a model judge?
- How do you handle factual uncertainty and missing evidence?
- How do you evaluate multilingual output if you are not fluent in the language?
- How do you turn recurring errors into better training data and regression tests?
- How do you maintain consistency at production volume?

## Strong answer structure
Use B-C-A-V-E: Business objective -> Choice of evaluation method -> Actions -> Validation -> Effect. Name the evidence, limitation and next test.
